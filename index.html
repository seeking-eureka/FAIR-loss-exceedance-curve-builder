    9. Things to Try

    • Set the stepper to 100 and press Re-run with new seed a few times. Watch P(loss > $5M) jump around the 10% line. Then do the same at 100K.
    • Switch the control off and the $5M point turns red. Switch it back on and lower the Vulnerability reduction to find the smallest reduction that keeps the firm inside appetite. Watch how the return on the control changes as you go.
    • Open Customer data breach and raise the secondary loss maximum from $15,000K to $40,000K. The annualized loss rises by under a fifth (about $2.3M to $2.7M), but the 1-in-100 year loss climbs by roughly two thirds (about $13M to $22M) and the worst simulated year doubles. Maximum estimates drive the tail far more than the average.
    • Switch Ransomware outage to Estimate LEF directly. The app pre-fills a range from TEF × Vulnerability, which is the top-down route O-RA allows. The control's TEF and Vulnerability reductions then apply to LEF as a whole, because there is no longer a separate Vulnerability to act on.
    • Switch the probability axis to Log. The 1% appetite point at $20M becomes readable, along with how thin the tail estimate is at low run counts.

    Input rules

    • Each range needs minimum ≤ most likely ≤ maximum. All three can be equal, which fixes the value.
    • TEF and LEF are events per year, 0 or more, up to 500. A maximum above 50 a year gets a soft warning.
    • Vulnerability and SLEF are probabilities from 0% to 100%.
    • Losses are in $K and must be 0 or more. A maximum more than 100 times the minimum gets a soft warning, since that implies an extreme tail.
    • The register holds 1 to 10 scenarios. The appetite curve holds 1 to 5 points. Larger losses can't have a higher tolerance than smaller ones.
    • Control reductions run from 0% to 90%, and the annual cost must be 0 or more.
